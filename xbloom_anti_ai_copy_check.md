# xBloom Anti-AI Copy Check

**Purpose:** A final editing pass for xBloom copy to catch language that feels AI-generated, templated, overly polished, generic, repetitive, or unnecessarily marketing-heavy.

Use this check across website copy, paid ads, email, social, product copy, and other customer-facing xBloom communications.

---

## 1. Avoid Obvious AI Writing Patterns

Before publishing, check for these common patterns:

- **No em dashes or en dashes.** Use commas, periods, parentheses, colons, or separate sentences.
- Avoid excessive use of **"not just X, but Y"** constructions.
- Avoid repetitive **three-part lists** created only for rhythm.
- Avoid repeatedly starting sentences with the same structure.
- Avoid unnecessary **"Whether you're X or Y..."** constructions unless the scenarios genuinely matter.
- Avoid rhetorical questions that do not add information.
- Avoid unnecessary setup before getting to the point.
- Avoid overly symmetrical sentences that sound written for effect rather than naturally spoken.
- Avoid stacked adjectives such as **"beautiful, intuitive, effortless, premium coffee experience."**
- Avoid overexplaining a simple benefit.
- Avoid repeating the same campaign phrase in the headline, subtitle, and body copy.
- Avoid turning every sentence into a slogan.

---

## 2. Watch for Common AI Vocabulary

Use these very selectively:

- Elevate
- Savor
- Unlock
- Seamless
- Effortless
- Curated
- Crafted
- Thoughtfully designed
- Designed to
- Reimagined
- Transform
- Experience
- Journey
- Discover a world of
- More than just
- At the heart of
- Whether you're
- From X to Y
- Perfect for
- Game changer
- Next level
- Redefine
- Exceptional
- Unparalleled
- Ultimate
- Amazing
- Unforgettable
- Must-have
- Treat yourself

Some of these words can be appropriate. The problem is using them because they sound like marketing rather than because they are the clearest word.

---

## 3. Avoid Fake Warmth

Do not force emotional language where a simple statement would sound more natural.

### Instead of:

> Savor every precious moment with a beautifully crafted cup.

### Prefer:

> A better cup for slower mornings.

Or simply:

> Coffee for slower mornings.

Warmth should come from relevance and specificity, not sentimental filler.

---

## 4. Avoid Generic Brand Language

Ask:

**Could another coffee brand, appliance company, or lifestyle brand use this exact sentence unchanged?**

If yes, rewrite it.

### Generic

> Designed to elevate your everyday coffee experience.

### More xBloom-specific

> Grind, brew, and repeat your favorite recipes from one machine.

Specific product truth almost always sounds more human than generic brand language.

---

## 5. Don't Manufacture a Story

Not every product needs:

- A journey
- A philosophy
- A moment
- A new chapter
- A ritual
- An emotional transformation
- A world to unlock

Sometimes the strongest copy is simply the useful fact.

If the product benefit is already interesting, let it stand on its own.

---

## 6. Prefer Natural Spoken Language

Read the copy aloud.

If it sounds like something nobody would naturally say, simplify it.

### Prefer:

> Get $100 toward holiday coffees.

### Over:

> Unlock $100 in value toward an unforgettable holiday coffee experience.

The goal is not to make copy casual. The goal is to make it sound like a real person wrote it.

---

## 7. Vary Sentence Rhythm Naturally

AI copy often produces several sentences with identical cadence and length.

Watch for patterns such as:

> Discover new flavors. Explore new origins. Experience better coffee.

Or:

> Made for better mornings. Made for better coffee. Made for you.

Mix short and slightly longer sentences when appropriate.

Do not force perfect symmetry.

---

## 8. Remove Filler

Watch for phrases such as:

- This season
- Like never before
- For every moment
- Made for you
- Something for everyone
- The perfect way to
- All season long
- And everything in between
- For whatever comes next
- Your new favorite
- Made to fit your lifestyle

Keep them only when they genuinely add meaning.

If deleting a phrase changes nothing, delete it.

---

## 9. Avoid Overusing Contrast Structures

AI copy often relies on contrast because it creates easy rhythm.

Common examples:

- Not just X, but Y
- More than X, it's Y
- From X to Y
- Whether X or Y
- X meets Y
- Where X meets Y

These structures are not banned, but they should not become a default writing habit.

Use them only when the contrast itself is meaningful.

---

## 10. Avoid Excessive Parallelism

Watch for copy that feels too perfectly balanced:

> Better beans. Better brewing. Better mornings.

> Grind smarter. Brew better. Drink happier.

> More control. More flavor. More possibilities.

This style can work occasionally in ad creative, but repeated use makes the brand sound templated.

One strong statement is usually better than three average ones.

---

## 11. Avoid Empty Premium Language

Words such as these often add tone without adding meaning:

- Premium
- Refined
- Sophisticated
- Luxurious
- Beautifully crafted
- Exceptional quality
- Meticulously designed
- Thoughtfully engineered

Use them only when supported by a concrete product fact.

### Weak:

> A thoughtfully engineered premium coffee experience.

### Better:

> 80 grind settings and precise recipe control in one machine.

---

## 12. Avoid Explaining the Obvious

Do not add a sentence merely because the copy feels short.

### Example

If the headline is:

> **2026 Advent Calendar**

And the subtitle already says:

> 24 coffees from the New Coffee World.

You do not need another sentence explaining that it is "a special way to count down to the holidays."

Short copy can be complete.

---

## 13. Avoid Repeating the Same Idea in Different Words

Check adjacent lines.

### Repetitive

**Headline:**  
Better Coffee at Home

**Subtitle:**  
Enjoy better coffee from the comfort of home.

**Body:**  
Make better coffee at home with xBloom.

This is one thought repeated three times.

Each layer should add something new.

---

## 14. Avoid Forced Cleverness

Do not keep a pun, rhyme, metaphor, or campaign phrase if it makes the message harder to understand.

Creative language should make the idea more memorable, not less clear.

When clarity and cleverness compete, clarity wins.

---

## 15. Keep Claims Grounded

AI-generated copy often escalates claims automatically.

Watch for:

- Best
- Most advanced
- Revolutionary
- Perfect
- Unmatched
- Unparalleled
- Never
- Always
- Guaranteed
- Changes everything

Use strong claims only when they are accurate, supportable, and appropriate.

Prefer concrete evidence over exaggerated adjectives.

---

## 16. Punctuation and Formatting Check

Before publishing:

- No em dashes.
- No en dashes.
- Avoid excessive exclamation marks.
- Avoid unnecessary semicolons.
- Avoid quotation marks around normal marketing phrases.
- Avoid excessive parentheses.
- Avoid ALL CAPS unless required by the creative format.
- Keep capitalization consistent.
- Use numerals for promotional values: **10%**, **$100**, **24 coffees**.
- Keep dates clear and concise.

---

## 17. Brand-Specific Words to Avoid

For xBloom, avoid or use very selectively:

- Ritual
- Elevate
- Savor
- Unlock
- Game changer
- Next level
- Coffee lover's dream
- Must-have
- Treat yourself
- Upgrade your ritual
- Transform your mornings
- Redefine coffee
- Discover a world of possibilities

These phrases tend to make otherwise specific xBloom copy sound generic.

---

## 18. Final Human Check

Before approving the copy, ask:

- Would a real xBloom team member naturally say this?
- Is there a simpler way to say it?
- Does every sentence add information?
- Does anything sound like generic ad copy?
- Are we trying too hard to sound clever?
- Are there unnecessary adjectives?
- Are there em dashes or en dashes?
- Is there repetitive sentence structure?
- Are we using a familiar AI phrase without realizing it?
- Could this line belong to almost any brand?
- Is the copy specific to the actual product, campaign, coffee, or offer?
- Does the copy sound confident enough to stop explaining itself?
- Can anything be deleted without losing meaning?

---

## 19. Final Rule

**If a sentence sounds polished but not specific, rewrite it. Specificity usually sounds more human.**
